Recommended position

The governed agent delivery control plane for client-facing technical work.

Keep the core engine horizontal and open source. Enter the market through software agencies, consultancies, managed service providers, and enterprise delivery teams that need AI agents to produce code, research, documents, and operational evidence for a customer or accountable stakeholder.

For teams delegating real client or product work to AI agents, Remote Sandbox Agents turns every request into an isolated, reviewable work order—with diffs, artifacts, approvals, handoffs, usage, and a durable record across models and runtimes.
Why this wedge
  1. Uses capabilities already present: board, sandbox attempts, snapshots, artifacts, handoff, review.
  2. Avoids the crowded generic assistant and customer-support markets.
  3. Creates an enterprise story around evidence, isolation, and accountable delivery.
  4. Supports open-source adoption plus paid governance, hosting, and client collaboration.

Where the current product can win

The strongest differentiation is not agent personalities. It is the accountable work-order lifecycle already embedded in the architecture.

USP

Fresh isolation per attempt

Each work order gets a fresh sandbox and immutable receiving envelope, reducing cross-task contamination and making policy easier to reason about.

  • Contrast with long-lived/shared desktops.
  • Backend-neutral sandbox SPI.
  • Explicit resource and egress ceilings.
USP

Proof-of-work package

A completed run can carry a real git diff, snapshot, declared artifacts, summary, tools, skills, model usage, and handoff lineage.

  • Customer-reviewable evidence.
  • Attempt-by-attempt provenance.
  • Reproducible delivery, not chat claims.
USP

Human-gated delivery

The harness owns external effects. Agents request handoffs and promotion; humans or deterministic policy approve consequential actions.

  • Attention/approval queue.
  • Client sign-off.
  • Earned autonomy over time.
USP

Open and model-neutral

Self-host with Postgres, Docker, and a provider key. Swap engines, sandboxes, snapshots, channels, skills, and promotion targets through seams.

  • No forced model bundle.
  • Customer-controlled data plane.
  • Community extension path.
USP

Cost per accepted deliverable

Attempt-first accounting can evolve beyond tokens into the metric buyers need: how much reliable, approved work reached completion.

  • Cost by attempt/task/profile.
  • Rework and acceptance rate.
  • Budget and ROI evidence.
USP

Client-safe workrooms

Project and tenant isolation, read-only client views, scoped artifacts, approvals, and audit can turn agent execution into a new service-delivery model.

  • Agency/consulting wedge.
  • Internal/external collaboration.
  • Reusable but governed profiles.

Product gaps to close for the recommended wedge

The commercial product should be built around trust and delivery quality, not simply a larger roster of agents.

G1P0

Tenant, project, client & identity boundary

  • Server-derived identity, SSO/OIDC, roles and membership.
  • Tenant-scoped persistence and artifact access.
  • Client reviewer role and secure read-only workroom.
  • Audit-grade actor and agent identity.
G2P0

Reliable execution and recovery

  • Lease heartbeat, stale-completion fencing, cancellation.
  • Durable attempt finalization and projection repair.
  • Concurrency/idempotency and split-topology restart proof.
  • Failure taxonomy and operator remediation.
G3P0

Trusted usage, cost & value ledger

  • Fix real Pi usage normalization and retain provider cost.
  • Model/profile/version/pricing provenance per attempt.
  • Cost per accepted deliverable, rework, approval rate.
  • Budgets and honest missing-data states.
G4P1

Integrations and credential broker

  • GitHub/GitLab, Linear/Jira, Slack/Teams first.
  • OAuth/secret references outside workspaces and prompts.
  • Per-connection scopes and per-agent policies.
  • Action ledger and approval class per integration.
G5P1

Attention and delivery experience

  • Default view: approvals, blockers, failures, then in-flight.
  • Task conversation without overbuilding token streaming.
  • Client-ready artifact/diff/report presentation.
  • Notifications and mobile approvals after access control.
G6P1

Profiles, skills and golden workflows

  • Immutable profile publishing and exact run pinning.
  • Delivery packs for code, research, QA, and incident review.
  • Evaluation suites and acceptance criteria per pack.
  • Safe marketplace/import model later.
G7P1

Enterprise observability and assurance

  • Task/attempt/tool/policy timeline and exports.
  • Success rate, latency, rework, violations, ROI dashboards.
  • Malicious-input, credential, egress, archive, IAM negatives.
  • Compliance evidence and deployment-specific claims.
G8P2

Hosted onboarding and operational product

  • Workspace provisioning and one-hour first value.
  • Guided project/agent setup and sample engagement.
  • Backups, upgrades, retention, support and status.
  • Remote sandbox provider only when local wedge is stable.

The first commercial product

Package a complete delivery loop rather than a blank agent builder.

Workflow 1

Engineering change room

Client request → scoped coder → reviewer → tests/diff → approval → patch/MR intent → client-visible evidence.

Workflow 2

Research and advisory room

Brief → research agent → document/chart artifacts → review checklist → human correction → approved client deliverable.

Workflow 3

Incident investigation room

Issue intake → reproduce/inspect → evidence timeline → proposed remediation → approval → post-mortem artifact. Add only after the first two are reliable.

North-star unit: an accepted deliverable, not a message, token, agent, or run. Optimize time-to-accepted-deliverable, cost per accepted deliverable, human review minutes, and rework rate.

Go-to-market strategy

Use the open-source service to earn technical trust; use a narrow, services-assisted commercial offer to discover the repeatable product.

Primary ICP

AI-forward delivery teams, 20–500 employees

  • Software agencies and development consultancies.
  • Managed service providers and internal platform teams.
  • Professional-services teams delivering research or technical work.
  • At least two agent workflows already in use or pilot.
  • Pain: inconsistent output, manual handoffs, no cost/provenance, client trust.
Buying group

Sell trust and throughput to three stakeholders

  • Economic buyer: founder, COO, VP Delivery.
  • Technical champion: CTO, Head of Engineering, AI Platform lead.
  • Risk reviewer: security, IT, legal/compliance.
  • Daily users: project manager, technical lead, reviewer.
  • Beneficiary: client or internal stakeholder receiving the work.
0–6 weeks

Discovery and wedge validation

Interview 25 delivery leaders; recruit 5 design partners; observe one real workflow each; baseline cycle time, rework, review time, failure recovery, and current agent cost. Test the phrase “client-safe agent delivery room.”

6–14 weeks

Concierge pilot

Deliver Engineering Change and Research Room as managed pilots. Founder-led onboarding, limited integrations, weekly reliability review. Target first accepted work order within 30 minutes and ten accepted deliverables in month one.

3–6 months

Repeatable product and proof

Publish 3 evidence-rich case studies, complete SSO/tenancy/usage ledger, add GitHub/GitLab + Linear/Jira + Slack/Teams, create a client reviewer view, and release versioned workflow packs.

6–12 months

Enterprise and ecosystem expansion

Add remote sandboxes, policy/export integrations, scheduled triggers, advanced budgets/evals, and compatibility with enterprise agent identity/control ecosystems. Explore OEM/API distribution after the operating model is stable.

Strategic risks and counter-moves

The opportunity is real, but the product must resist three seductive failure modes.

Risk

Feature-chasing generalists

Trying to match 70+ integrations, mobile chat, memory, voice, schedules, and every agent role before proving a buyer.

Counter: two golden workflows, four integrations, one ICP, measurable accepted deliverables.

Risk

Infrastructure without a product

A technically excellent sandbox/control plane can remain a developer component with no urgent buyer outcome.

Counter: package client delivery rooms, onboarding, workflow packs, review UX, and business metrics.

Risk

Enterprise claims before proof

Self-hosting and isolation are not enough without identity, authorization, recovery, audit, live canaries, and operational support.

Counter: publish explicit readiness levels and evidence; sell design partnerships before “enterprise-ready.”
